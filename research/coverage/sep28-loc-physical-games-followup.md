# Library of Congress physical-game follow-up — 2026-09-28

Three distinct physical games were reviewed against the 5,250-identity
directory released at `85c0c31f7d7eed71d7ab6b6b32e41ccc86da9539`.
The three accepted records in [publisher-identities.json](publisher-identities.json)
raise the local count to 5,253. These are **identity-only** additions: no
starting-player rule, modern edition correspondence, BoardGameGeek ID or
image is approved for publication.

| Original item | Decision | Physical and publisher evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [*Middleton's New geographical game of a tour through England and Wales*](https://www.loc.gov/item/85695490/) | Accept M. Middleton's 1820 boxed issue | The [original board, printed directions and box](https://tile.loc.gov/image-services/iiif/service:gmd:gmd5:g5751:g5751a:ct006184/full/pct:25/0/default.jpg) show the title, M. Middleton London imprint and a playable map route. The Library catalogs a 77-piece wooden jigsaw, directions, counters and die (the latter two now missing). It says the map was published May 1820. This is a single boxed issue, not a blanket identity for later geographic games. |
| [*The traveller's tour through the United States*](https://www.loc.gov/item/2017585497/) | Accept F. & R. Lockwood's 1822 U.S. issue | The [original recto](https://tile.loc.gov/image-services/iiif/service:gmd:gmd370:g3701:g3701a:ct011568r/full/pct:25/0/default.jpg) prints the game title, rules, 139 numbered places and 1822 Lockwood imprint; the [verso](https://tile.loc.gov/image-services/iiif/service:gmd:gmd370:g3701:g3701a:ct011568v/full/pct:12.5/0/default.jpg) bears the matching publisher label. The catalog describes a hand-colored cardboard-mounted physical map. Lockwood's later Europe and world tours are separate leads. |
| [*Los charros contrabandistas. Juego de dados*](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/99615952/) | Accept one Antonio Vanegas Arroyo dice-game print | The [digital image from the original print](https://cdn.loc.gov/service/pnp/ppmsc/03400/03448v.jpg) shows the title, 64-space game route, `Explicacion` rules panel and `Imprenta de A. Vanegas Arroyo, Mexico` imprint. The Library catalogs the physical sheet and credits José Guadalupe Posada as artist. Its date is only bounded to 1890–1913, so no exact year is claimed. |
| [*Snake game rules*](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2007675305/) | Hold | The [original board](https://cdn.loc.gov/service/pnp/pga/03300/03362v.jpg) is plainly playable, but the Library lists it as unattributed and the inspected image does not establish a publisher. The registry's accepted identity policy requires a primary publisher reference. |

The Library's Geography and Map Division item pages allow reuse absent a
specific contrary advisory. The Vanegas Arroyo print has no known U.S.
reproduction restriction, with possible limits elsewhere. This site ships
only basic bibliographic facts, scope and evidence descriptions; the scanned
art and game-rule text are not copied. Five ignored source images were opened
for visual inspection. The [batch validator](../../scripts/prepare-loc-physical-games-batch.mjs)
re-fetches four accepted source URLs and compares exact byte counts and
SHA-256 hashes. The extra snake image remains an unregistered research lead.

Exact and normalized accepted names were absent from the active research and
site source projection before insertion. Each record excludes later printings,
translations and similarly named games. The date and publisher assertions
are limited to what the original artifacts and linked Library catalog records
establish.
