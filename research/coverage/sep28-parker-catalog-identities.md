# Parker Brothers illustrated catalog identities — 2026-09-28

Five physical Parker Brothers products in its [*Illustrated Catalogue of
Standard Games*](https://gamesandpuzzles.org/images/trade-catalogs/parker-brothers/1889_Parker_Brothers_catalog_-Strong_Parker_Brothers_Inc._1889_95033143-from_TheStrong_%28handmarked_1892_incorrectly%29_-prep_2025-01-02_6MB.pdf)
were accepted as scoped, identity-only records. The scan of this original
publisher catalog is hosted in the American Game & Puzzle Collectors trade
catalog collection. It is indexed as 1889, but the printed catalog pages do
not show a date. None of these records assigns an exact manufacture year.

| Catalog product | Decision and direct evidence |
| --- | --- |
| *The Soldier Boy* | Accept the boxed folding-board issue on printed page 3 (PDF page 3). Publisher art depicts the military-career board; the text lists metal soldier playing pieces and a spinning indicator. |
| *Innocence Abroad* | Accept the boxed travel board on printed page 4 (PDF page 4). A labeled box illustration and description of a large folding board, utensils and multi-route journey establish the product. |
| *Rex* | Accept the single-board boxed issue on printed page 5 (PDF page 4). The board illustration has the Parker Bros. imprint; the description identifies maple pieces, a spinner and a $1.00 price. |
| *Progressive Rex* | Accept a separate four-board boxed set on printed page 5 (PDF page 4). The catalog gives it a separate heading, four boards, complete utensils and a $3.50 price. Shared rules or identical components with the single-board product are unproved. |
| *The Game of War* | Accept the boxed battlefield-board issue on printed page 9 (PDF page 6). The publisher illustrates its connected-point board and specifies maple playing pieces, directions and a 75-cent price. This is scoped away from unrelated games with the generic title. |

The publisher introduction on printed page 2 (PDF page 3) identifies Parker
Brothers of Salem as the games publisher. Exact and normalized product names
were absent from the active identity datasets before insertion; the unrelated
*U.S.N.: The Game of War in the Pacific, 1941–43* was the only partial title
match found. *The Office Boy*, illustrated on printed page 8, was already
accepted from a separate original board held by the Library of Congress and
was not added again. Catalog products outside this bounded batch, including
the *Railroad Game* and *Garrison Game*, remain research leads.

No starting-player rule, later-edition correspondence, BoardGameGeek ID, or
publisher art is transferred to the site. The [batch validator](../../scripts/prepare-parker-catalog-identities.mjs)
pins the 6,015,474-byte PDF by SHA-256

and validates the five accepted record revisions.
